Company

Building the default independent safety layer for AI agents

Sentinel AI is an early-stage company. This page lays out how we think about the market, how we plan to go to market, and what we still need to prove — deliberately, not as settled fact.

Investment logic

Four layers of the thesis

📈

Structural demand

Agents are moving from generating content to executing action. Enterprises must build safety controls that match the authority they're granting.

🚪

A clear wedge

Enterprise buyers lack their own AI red team, and a vendor grading its own agent is a structural conflict of interest — third-party verification has a natural reason to be purchased.

🔁

A path to recurring revenue

Assurance isn't the end point — it's the entry to Guard and Continuous Assurance, turning project revenue into ongoing subscription.

🧠

A compounding data & method moat

Every Assurance engagement and every Guard deployment strengthens the next one — the flywheel described on our product page.

Go-to-market

Land and expand

We land with a high-value, high-touch independent Assurance engagement on a permission-clear, high-risk agent project. That engagement builds trust and captures the threat model, business boundaries and attack evidence needed to expand into Guard for production/pre-production environments, converting into subscription and re-testing revenue.

Land

First customers are enterprises actively procuring or deploying high-authority agents — starting with finance, procurement and internal-tool workflows. Model vendors and integrators can be channel partners later, without diluting Sentinel's position as an independent third party.

Expand

Threat models, attack traces and business boundaries captured during Assurance become the foundation for deploying Guard and Continuous Assurance — converting one-time project revenue into recurring, usage-based subscription.

Organization

Early on, the CEO leads customer discovery, product definition and the first deals; the CTO and Chief Scientist drive technical pre-sales and delivery. We're building ToB security sales, product engineering and China market execution before scaling the commercial team.

Business model

From project revenue to safety infrastructure

We don't yet have customer-validated pricing, ACV, gross margin or sales-cycle data. Our investor model will be rebuilt on real numbers after the first 5–10 design partnerships / paid projects, rather than a top-down market-size assumption.

Shenzhen & China

An industrial validation base, not a pivot

Shenzhen is an important proving ground and China landing base — not a change to our initial beachhead. We're still prioritizing procurement/finance agents and internal-tool workflow agents first, because their permission boundaries are clear and the demo and customer value are easy to quantify. On that foundation, Shenzhen's robotics, smart manufacturing, drone and outbound-trade companies give us a distinctive path toward physical-action security and cross-border expansion.

Phase 1

Validate high-authority software agent scenarios with Shenzhen enterprises — procurement, finance, internal tool calls, cross-border workflows — forming reusable Assurance + Guard case studies.

Phase 2

Once the core platform matures, extend into robotics, drones and smart manufacturing — device control, material scheduling and physical action safety — as an extension of Sentinel's agent security capability, not an early second product line.

Goal: 3–5 design partnerships with Shenzhen enterprises, prioritizing partners that produce publicly shareable case studies or reusable industry templates. Sensitive enterprise data is supported via private / on-premise deployment.

What we still need to prove

Key assumptions we're de-risking

As an early-stage company, these are the open questions to treat as the next 6–12 months of work — not settled facts.

  • →Can runtime Guard make reliable calls on complex agent behavior at low enough latency and false-positive rate?
  • →Does AAL stay sufficiently repeatable and interpretable across different agents, labs and time periods?
  • →Who actually owns budget — CISO, CIO, AI platform team, procurement/risk, or the business owner?
  • →Will enterprises buy independent verification and continuous safety software directly, or expect it bundled into an existing security vendor?
  • →University-origin IP, student projects, open-source components and future company IP boundaries need to be clarified ahead of fundraising.
