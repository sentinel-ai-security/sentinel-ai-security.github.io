Team

Three research disciplines, one technical loop

Securing an agent lifecycle means answering four questions: how do you quantify how safe it is, how do you find out why it isn't, how do you stop dangerous behavior, and how do you prove a fix actually worked? Those map directly onto our three founders' complementary research backgrounds — Measure → Attack & Attribute → Protect & Re-verify.

WX

Dr. Wenkai Xu

Co-founder & Chief Scientist — Measure Statistics & Machine Learning, University of Warwick

Wenkai leads Sentinel's scientific methodology, statistical verification, and the AAL methodology itself. His research focuses on statistical hypothesis testing, distribution comparison, and interpretable machine learning — the work that turns "we ran a lot of attacks" into "we can quantify how safe this agent is under a defined threat model." That's the mathematical foundation behind Sentinel's statistical safety verification and AAL scoring.

LW

Dr. Lichao Wu

Co-founder & CTO — Attack & Attribute Cyber Security, University of Bristol

Lichao owns Sentinel's overall technical architecture, AI security capability, and product engineering. His research spans adversarial modeling, systems security, and mechanistic analysis of intelligent-system vulnerabilities — not just how to attack an agent, but why the attack succeeds, and whether the failure lives in the input, the model internals, the tool call, or the permission boundary. That underpins both Assurance's independent red teaming and Guard's real-time anomaly detection.

USENIX Security NDSS CHES NDSS'26 Distinguished Paper Award
NL

Dr. Nan Lu

Co-founder & CEO — Protect & Re-verify Artificial Intelligence & Trustworthy AI, University of Bristol

Nan leads overall strategy, product, go-to-market, customers and fundraising. Her research covers robust learning under distribution shift, weak supervision, and machine unlearning — the discipline of how to restore an AI system to safety after a risk is found, and prove it's trustworthy again. That drives Sentinel's move from finding problems to intervention, runtime protection, and continuous re-verification.

What we're building toward

Not a one-time scanner — a compounding trust loop

Competitors can copy a single attack technique, bolt on a guardrail, or generate a safety report. What we're accumulating is the full loop between attack, behavior, risk, intervention and re-verification — and it strengthens with every enterprise agent that connects to the platform.
