NeST: making LLMs safer by tuning a few neurons, accepted at IEEE S&P 2027

Our earlier work showed that a small set of neurons holds up an LLM's safety. NeST turns that around: tune only those neurons, and you get close to the protection of full fine-tuning at a fraction of the cost.

By the Sentinel Intelligence team · 2027 · Read the paper on arXiv ↗
← Back to News

Our paper, "NeST: Neuron Selective Tuning for LLM Safety," has been accepted at the IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy (S&P) 2027, one of the top four venues in computer security. The work is co-authored by Sentinel Intelligence co-founder Lichao Wu, together with Sasha Behrouzi, Mohamadreza Rostami, and Ahmad-Reza Sadeghi (Technical University of Darmstadt).

What NeST does

Making a model refuse harmful requests usually means costly fine-tuning, and cheaper methods like LoRA give inconsistent safety gains. NeST finds the feed-forward neurons that matter for safety, groups neurons that behave alike, and trains a shared update for each group while the rest of the model stays frozen.

  • Tested across 14 open-weight language and multimodal models.
  • On text-only models, average jailbreak success drops from 44.5% to 1.1%.
  • Trains about 0.4 million parameters on average, far fewer than full fine-tuning, while coming close to its robustness.
  • Trained only on plain harmful and benign prompts, with no jailbreak-specific data, yet it holds up against a wide range of jailbreaks.
  • The updates are folded back into the model's weights, so there's no extra cost when the model runs.

The generalization result is the one worth sitting with. A defense that never saw a jailbreak during training still resists them, which suggests it's fixing the underlying safety mechanism rather than patching specific attacks.

Our take

Finding a weakness is half the job; the other half is fixing it without breaking everything else. NeST builds on the same neuron-level understanding as our attack work, NeuroStrike and GateBreaker, and uses it for defense. Knowing exactly where safety lives inside a model is what makes both precise testing and precise repair possible.
